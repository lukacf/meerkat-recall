Each publishable assertion needs one exact, contiguous quote from the selected immutable text window. The independent verifier sees this quote and the complete claim, not the rest of the document. Select enough surrounding text to support the subject, predicate, object or typed literal, and any claimed attribution, scope, modality, time, or event title. When a sentence uses a pronoun, an unnamed request, or another reference to an earlier sentence, include the actual antecedent in the same quote. A short quote that omits the claimed subject or a necessary condition is incomplete evidence even when the full document supplies that context.

Expand the quote only within the original selected text, with matching UTF-8 byte offsets. Preserve every intervening byte; do not concatenate separated excerpts, paraphrase, add ellipses, or bring in another document. A wider quote is not automatic support: ambiguous references and unsupported interpretations remain unsupported. Never invent a subject from a document title, source key, path, caller metadata, or an existing entity lookup to repair missing textual support. Such metadata can help locate evidence but cannot replace it. Keep real source-backed qualifications and denials in the complete proposal; do not remove them merely to obtain approval. Missing evidence and a negative verifier decision cannot authorize publication.

Distinguish a fact about stated or proposed content from a claim that the described event or obligation actually holds. A quote can establish what a draft proposes, what an instruction requests, or what a speaker conditionally predicts. Keep that attribution and modality explicit in the complete subject, predicate and literal; do not turn it into an operative obligation or completed event. Use a document or proposal label as the subject only when the quoted text itself supplies that referent. An unnamed rejection does not identify who rejected the proposal.

Keep denials within their stated scope. Saying that one document is rejected, unsigned, superseded, or not another document does not establish which terms the other document contains. Distinct documents can share a term. Do not strengthen a document-status qualification into a claim that an unquoted agreement, policy or source lacks the same content. Preserve the supported proposed content and its status together without inventing that additional exclusion; a repair must reconsider the complete proposal, not publish only the assertions that previously passed.
